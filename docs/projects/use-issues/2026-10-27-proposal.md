# The Issue Tracker skill family: proposal

This document captures state as of 2026-10-27. It is the living artifact of
the home-in that is scoping the family (`agent-tooling-u9n.1`, scope the
Issue Tracker skill-family split), rewritten in place at each point where a
line of questioning closes, and it becomes this project's `design.md` as the
work ships. Until an `index.md` exists beside it, the scoping Issue holds the
current status.

## Context

Every issue-related skill, script, and wrapper in this repo is being
redesigned as one family: a generic core (how this ecosystem files, breaks
down, links, works, and closes Issues) with thin platform layers for Beads,
GitHub Issues, and Forgejo issues, split for progressive disclosure so a
session loads only what its use case needs. For this design, "use-issues"
names that whole tree, setup and triage included.

The guidance being replaced was written over many sessions, some of it
imported from a predecessor with no history, and each agent moved the last
one's text a small, sensible step away from what the maintainer meant. The
scoping procedure recovers the original intent: extract every principle with
its provenance (done; see the
[extraction audit](../../audits/2026-10-26-issue-guidance-extraction/index.md)),
have the maintainer approve every requirement in their own words (done; the
approved set is on the scoping Issue), home in on the open topics (in
progress; this document), then plan and break down.

The family is also the pilot for restructuring every skill in the repo for
progressive disclosure (`agent-tooling-jat`), which waits on it.

## Problem

Six topics were left open by the requirements review:

1. Ownership: how much judgement a project delegates to agents, and how that
   is expressed. **Settled**; see Decisions.
2. The filing bar, the stage model, and acceptance versus success criteria,
   as one knot.
3. The shape of the skill family and its progressive-disclosure split.
4. Triage: its autonomy boundary, the ledger, and priority-scaled staleness.
5. Whether a discovered Issue links to the Issue that surfaced it.
6. The Issue reference form for commits and PRs.

## Success criteria

- Every rule in the family traces to wording the maintainer approved.
- One generic core; platform skills hold only what their platform makes
  different, with Beads treated as the exception and the forges standardized
  together.
- A session loads only what its use case needs.
- One stage model and one filing bar that hold on every tracker.
- A repo is made fully agent-owned by changing grants, never by rewriting
  skills or tools.
- Procedures are actor-neutral: they say what somebody must do, and who does
  it is decided elsewhere.

## Decisions

### Delegation is expressed as Permission rules, on situation-split Verbs

A Permission rule grants an operation; the oversight an operation needs
varies by situation. The resolution is to make each situation a project may
grant separately its own invocation, so grants can tell them apart, and to
keep policy about who may act out of the skills. Recorded as
[ADR 0017](../../adr/0017-delegation-is-expressed-as-permission-rules-on-situation-split-verbs.md)
(delegation as Permission rules on situation-split Verbs), and as an
invariant on the lexicon's Verb entry. The requirements, in the maintainer's
approved wording:

> **R45.** When one Verb applies in several situations that call for
> different levels of delegation, the Verb is augmented or split so that
> each situation is a different invocation, and each invocation can receive
> its own Permission rule. Where no honest choice of invocation can separate
> the situations, the Verb is granted as its most demanding situation.

> **R46.** Changing or destroying existing data requires more delegation
> than purely additive change. Within that, less judgement is needed when
> the action merely executes an existing, already-authorized plan (forming
> Issues from a design document's scope, say), and more when the agent is
> making the decision itself rather than carrying one out (inferring a new
> overarching project and reparenting existing Issues under it). We
> therefore expose second versions of high-judgement Verbs that take the
> source evidence justifying each change, recorded for provenance audit. A
> project that delegates less (through `allow` Permission rules) can then
> open these operations to agents in their source-providing form while
> withholding the blanket form that needs no justification. The goal is
> agent accountability and aligned incentives, not foolproof adherence:
> false claims are detectable and correctable after the fact. This pattern
> is not a fit for dangerous operations whose policy must be foolproof;
> those are gated on the operation itself.

> **R48.** Policy (who may do what) lives in Permission rules, and leaves
> the skills wherever the system can express it as a grant: if the grant
> exists, the agent does the act; if not, the call is denied or asked, and
> that is the answer. Only where Permission rules cannot separate the
> situations does a skill say which situation calls for which invocation.
> Discipline (when an act is correct, what it must carry, what to check
> first) stays in the skills; it is not a grant and never says who may act.

Which operations are gated on the operation itself (`ask` or `deny`, no
provenance form) is a judgement made when a Wrapper's tiers are designed,
weighing how much harm and toil a wrong move costs to correct, how long it
would likely go undetected, and what compounds in the meantime. An effect
that cannot be corrected at all is always gated this way; that is the floor,
not the test.

What follows for the open Issues on agent authority:

- **Closing** is a Verb with a mandatory reason, granted per repo; closing
  one's own finished work is the routine case. Triage's "every close is a
  proposal" becomes that repo's grant, not a rule.
- **Rewriting bodies**: the filer refines what they filed (R15); whoever
  works an Issue rewrites its description as state (R33); rewriting an
  Issue's scope on the agent's own inference is R46's high-judgement case.
- **The search-before-filing cases (R11)**: linking a dependency, adding
  context to an existing Issue, and filing under an existing parent are
  additive or conforming, so routine; filing a new Issue as parent of an
  existing one reparents existing work on inference, so it takes the
  source form or an `ask`; a contradiction is reported; partial overlap is
  talked through attended and proposed in the report unattended; a closed
  match is a reopen, which sits in a higher tier.
- **Regression** (moving status backwards, R30) is a reason-carrying Verb
  like close, granted per repo.
- **Sub-agents may not claim or close** on Beads because a claim means
  "this is mine" and a shared Actor ID cannot say whose. The lexicon's
  Actor ID invariant ("never shared by two Actors") stands; today's sharing
  is a Known gap, closed when sub-agents get distinct Actor IDs.

bdw, a pass-through over bd, gets ground-up Verb design rather than a
retrofit, since bd's own syntax has no slot for a situation or a source.

### Every forge operation names its Forge identity

On Forgejo agents already act as a machine account; on GitHub they acted as
the maintainer, so an agent's act was recorded as the maintainer's. Every
forge Verb now takes a Forge identity (a lexicon term: an account on a
forge, registered by name in Machine Config) as its argument directly after
the repo, on every operation, and every write is marked with the Actor ID.
Which identities a repo grants is a Permission rule, so a repo can let
agents act as the machine account for autonomous work and as the maintainer
for assisted work, and a third-party repo, where no machine account exists,
grants only the maintainer's. Recorded as
[ADR 0018](../../adr/0018-every-forge-operation-names-its-forge-identity.md)
(every forge operation names its Forge identity).

> **R9 (revised).** Every state change has exactly one recorded owner. The
> identity an agent acts under is one the Permission rules grant for that
> repo, named by the agent and resolved by the Wrapper, never supplied as a
> raw account. The Actor ID is recorded on every write, whatever identity
> it ran under; when an agent acts under a person's account, that record is
> what keeps its acts distinguishable from the person's.

> **R47.** Forge Verbs take the identity as an argument on every operation,
> reads included, in the same position: after the repo, before anything
> else, so Permission rules can grant each identity per repo at the lowest
> cardinality. An identity is a name resolved against the forge Wrapper's
> Machine Config, which agents can neither read nor write; each forge keeps
> its own list, since a GitHub identity and a Forgejo identity are
> different things.

### Attended and unattended sessions

A session is unattended exactly when its prompt says so; otherwise a person
may be asked and is expected to answer, however the session was launched.
The Actor ID's launch prefix records how a session started, not whether it
is attended. In an Unattended session, an act that would need a person's
approval is written up as a proposal in the report instead of attempted.
Both terms are in the lexicon; a single-fact Skill will be the one home for
the operating rule (`agent-tooling-in76`).

### Not a new concept: the delegation level

The home-in considered a named per-project "delegation level" and rejected
it: with R45 to R48, what a project delegates is exactly what its Permission
rules grant, and a second noun would be a second place for the same fact.
When a generalized capability model arrives, the rewrite is one sentence in
the Permission rule entry.
