# The Issue Tracker skill family: proposal

This document captures state as of 2026-11-05. For current state, see the
`index.md` beside it once the family ships; this proposal becomes the
project's `design.md` then. Capitalized terms (Verb, Wrapper, Actor ID,
Forge identity, Branch Issue, Tracker Schema, Issue State Diagram, and the
rest) are defined in [`LEXICON.md`](../../../LEXICON.md). The requirements
cited by number (R1 to R57) are in the appendix, in the maintainer's
approved wording.

## Context

Every issue-related skill, script, and wrapper in this repo becomes one
family: a generic core (how this ecosystem files, breaks down, links,
works, and closes Issues) with thin platform layers for Beads, GitHub
Issues, and Forgejo issues, split for progressive disclosure so a session
loads only what its use case needs. For this design, "use-issues" names
that whole tree, setup and triage included.

The guidance being replaced was written over many sessions, some of it
imported from a predecessor with no history, and each agent moved the last
one's text a small, sensible step away from what the maintainer meant. This
design recovers the original intent: every principle was extracted with its
provenance (the
[extraction audit](../../audits/2026-10-26-issue-guidance-extraction/index.md)),
every requirement was approved by the maintainer in their own words, and
the six questions the requirements left open were settled by interrogation
(`agent-tooling-u9n.1`, scope the Issue Tracker skill-family split, holds
the rulings and their reasons).

The family is also the pilot for restructuring every skill in the repo for
progressive disclosure (`agent-tooling-jat`), which waits on it.

## Scope

In scope: the generic skills, the platform skills (use-bd, use-github,
use-forgejo as far as issues go), the issue Verbs of ghw, fjw, and bdw, the
triage scripts, and setup. Out of scope, following later under their own
epics once the grammar is proven: the PR Verbs and gitw.

## Success criteria

- Every rule in the family traces to wording the maintainer approved.
- One generic core; platform skills hold only what their platform makes
  different, with Beads treated as the exception and the forges
  standardized together.
- A session loads only what its use case needs.
- One filing bar that holds on every tracker, and a lifecycle each repo can
  own without any skill knowing its states.
- A repo is made fully agent-owned by changing grants, never by rewriting
  skills or tools.
- Procedures are actor-neutral: they say what somebody must do, and who does
  it is decided elsewhere.

## Design

### Delegation is expressed as Permission rules, on situation-split Verbs

A Permission rule grants an operation; the oversight an operation needs
varies by situation. Reparenting Issues under an approved design is
routine; inferring a new project and reparenting existing Issues under it
is scoping. The resolution is to make each situation a project may grant
separately its own invocation, so grants can tell them apart, and to keep
policy about who may act out of the skills (R45, R46, R48;
[ADR 0017](../../adr/0017-delegation-is-expressed-as-permission-rules-on-situation-split-verbs.md),
delegation as Permission rules on situation-split Verbs; an invariant on
the lexicon's Verb entry).

The two-by-two behind R46: an act is additive or it alters existing data,
and it conforms to a decision made elsewhere or it makes the decision on
the agent's own inference. Only "alters existing, on its own inference"
is the high-judgement case, and that is the case that gets a
source-providing form of the Verb (`per <source>`, naming the design
document, plan, or existing parent the change conforms to). Filing a new
Leaf Issue on one's own inference is additive and needs no source (R14).

Which operations are gated on the operation itself (`ask` or `deny`, no
provenance form) is a judgement made when a Wrapper's tiers are designed,
weighing how much harm and toil a wrong move costs to correct, how long it
would likely go undetected, and what compounds in the meantime. An effect
that cannot be corrected at all is always gated this way; that is the floor,
not the test (R36).

What follows for agent authority, with no rule about agents:

- **Closing** is a Verb with a mandatory reason (R35), granted per repo;
  closing one's own finished work is the routine case. Triage's "every
  close is a proposal" is that repo's grant, not a rule.
- **Rewriting bodies**: the filer refines what they filed (R15); whoever
  works an Issue rewrites its description as state (R33); rewriting an
  Issue's scope on the agent's own inference is R46's high-judgement case.
- **The search-before-filing cases (R11)**: linking a dependency, adding
  context to an existing Issue, and filing under an existing parent are
  additive or conforming, so routine; filing a new Issue as parent of an
  existing one reparents existing work on inference, so it takes the
  source form or an `ask`; a contradiction is reported; partial overlap is
  talked through attended and proposed in the report unattended; a closed
  match is a reopen, which sits in a higher tier.
- **Regression** (moving an Issue back to an earlier Issue State, R30) is a
  reason-carrying Verb like close, granted per repo.
- **Sub-agents may not claim or close** on Beads because a claim means
  "this is mine" and a shared Actor ID cannot say whose (R10). The
  lexicon's Actor ID invariant ("never shared by two Actors") stands;
  today's sharing is a Known gap on the use-bd and bdw projects
  (`agent-tooling-i4e`), closed when sub-agents get distinct Actor IDs.

bdw, a pass-through over bd, gets ground-up Verb design rather than a
retrofit, since bd's own syntax has no slot for a situation or a source.

### Every forge operation names its Forge identity

On Forgejo agents already act as a machine account; on GitHub they acted as
the maintainer, so an agent's act was recorded as the maintainer's. Every
forge Verb takes a Forge identity as its argument directly after the repo,
on every operation, and every write is marked with the Actor ID (R9, R47;
[ADR 0018](../../adr/0018-every-forge-operation-names-its-forge-identity.md),
every forge operation names its Forge identity). The identity answers
"with whose authority"; the Actor ID answers "whose hands". Which
identities a repo grants is a Permission rule, so a repo can let agents act
as the machine account for autonomous work and as the maintainer for
assisted work, and a third-party repo, where no machine account exists,
grants only the maintainer's. Acting under a person's account is a risk
that person accepts by granting it; the Actor ID marker is what keeps those
acts distinguishable.

### Attended and unattended sessions

A session is unattended exactly when its prompt says so; otherwise a person
may be asked and is expected to answer, however the session was launched.
The Actor ID's launch prefix records how a session started, not whether it
is attended. In an Unattended session, an act that would need a person's
approval is written up as a proposal in the report instead of attempted.
Both terms are in the lexicon, and the `know-unattended` Knowledge Skill is
the one home for the operating rule.

### The filing bar is one minimum on every tracker

The bar is R49: a title that states the outcome; a body that says what is
known and what is not, with no placeholder text; and an Issue Kind, an
Issue Priority, and the entry Issue State, each set as best the filer can.
More is welcome so long as it states what is known rather than presuming
design or implementation. Everything beyond the minimum (a pointer,
reproduction steps, criteria) is a condition on a later Issue Transition.

The invariant that no Issue lacks a priority or a state is kept by the bar
itself: an Issue with a title and one honest sentence is correctly in the
entry state. Beads defaults a priority and a status on every Issue, so the
invariant cannot be broken there, but a defaulted priority is the tool's
guess, not the filer's, so "set as best you can" still applies; on the
forges nothing defaults, so the filing Verb sets them. The bar does not
differ by tracker; only the mechanics do.

### Lifecycle: the Tracker Schema and its Issue State Diagram

Every repo has a Tracker Schema (its Issue Kinds, Issue Priorities, Issue
Label Schema, and Issue State Diagram), written as a file in the repo,
copied from the shipped default with the revision it came from, so every
edit is that repo's customization (R50;
[ADR 0020](../../adr/0020-every-repo-carries-its-own-copy-of-the-tracker-schema.md),
every repo carries its own copy of the Tracker Schema). The Issue State
Diagram holds every state meaning and every transition condition in
natural language, as mini-prompts the repo owner writes; the only structure
is whether a state is open or closed; the skills say nothing about states
except "judge every move against the repo's Issue State Diagram"; the
ready queue is defined by the agent as the unclaimed, unblocked Issues in
the states it works
([ADR 0019](../../adr/0019-issue-lifecycle-semantics-live-in-the-issue-state-diagram.md),
lifecycle semantics live in the Issue State Diagram). The same file is the
one home for the schema's other parts, which answers `agent-tooling-wx5`
(one canonical per-repo config home) for this piece.

The default Issue State Diagram, which slice 1 of the plan writes:

- Six open states, Needs Triage, Needs Scope, Needs Eng Design, Needs Impl,
  In progress, In review, and one closed state whose reason says done,
  rejected, duplicate, or superseded (R35). "Done" and "Rejected" are not
  attention states; they are closed plus a reason, which is what Beads and
  GitHub both record natively.
- Out of Needs Triage: the universal bar met, conforming labels, and a
  pointer (a code location, an observable behavior); for a bug, what was
  observed and what was expected.
- Out of Needs Scope: the problem is defined, not the solution (R31a:
  motivation, beneficiary, criteria, out of scope, constraints,
  adjacencies); a Branch Issue states its success criteria and a Leaf Issue
  its acceptance criteria, either may carry the other as well; for a bug,
  how to reproduce it, or for an intermittent one what was observed, how
  often, and under what conditions, enough that a debugger knows where to
  start. Scoping a bug is reproducing it, so there is no separate
  investigation state; a repo that works that way adds one to its diagram.
- Out of Needs Eng Design: an implementer could proceed without further
  design input (R31b).
- A claim moves Needs Impl to In progress and otherwise leaves the state as
  it is; a regression is a transition like any other, whose condition is
  that an earlier bar turned out not to be met (R30).
- Each priority level declares its staleness threshold, with the values
  triage-issues uses today as the defaults.

R31a and R31b keep their approved wording; their home is the default
diagram, not the skills. "Blocked" and "waiting on a human" are not states:
a blocking dependency blocks in every state (R27's stage-aware blocking is
a later refinement the diagram makes possible), and waiting on a human is
a gate (R28).

Criteria by kind are lexicon invariants: a Branch Issue states success
criteria, which the pass that verifies it checks against the outcome (its
verifying pass, per
[ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-branch-issues-are-worked-in-passes.md), Branch
Issues are worked in passes); a Leaf Issue states acceptance criteria and
is closed when they hold.

### Claims, ready, and assignee on a forge

On a forge, a claim assigns the Issue to the Forge identity the Verb runs
under and leaves a comment carrying the Actor ID; a release does the
reverse after the pick-up is written; whether a claim also moves the Issue
State is the diagram's call (R51). One person or agent advances an Issue
at a time, so a claim is made at any stage, not only implementation, and
what is learned is appended to the Issue it belongs to regardless of who
filed or holds it (R32). There is no queue mechanism on a forge, only the
query ([ADR 0011](../../adr/0011-beads-for-swarmed-work-forge-issues-for-long-lived-work.md),
Beads for swarmed work and forge issues for long-lived work, stays as it
is); the assignee is the lease, and forge contention is low enough for
that. bd's own claim sets its status to `in_progress` natively, so bdw's
ground-up Verb design maps that onto the diagram (a claim in Needs Scope
leaves the state alone); that is Beads residue for slice 4, not a generic
rule.

### The shape: a graph of moment skills, self-documenting wrappers, and failsafe platform skills

The generic skills are split by the moment at which an agent needs them,
the fundamentals are a Knowledge Skill, and there is no table-of-contents
skill: the edges of the graph are the skill descriptions the harness lists
in every session, pointers in skill text at the moment of need, and the
wrappers' help and refusals (R52;
[ADR 0021](../../adr/0021-issue-skills-are-split-by-moment-of-need.md),
issue skills split by moment of need). How to use a platform's tool lives
in the tool, and a platform skill is a failsafe pointer for an agent that
searches tool-first (R53;
[ADR 0022](../../adr/0022-platform-usage-lives-in-the-wrappers-help.md),
platform usage lives in the wrapper's help). Every wrapper's issue Verbs
share one top-level grammar, adopted per wrapper as each is built or
rebuilt (R54;
[ADR 0023](../../adr/0023-issue-verbs-share-one-top-level-grammar.md),
one top-level grammar across wrappers).

The tree:

- **Knowledge:** `know-issues`. The fundamentals, distilled into the
  description: one writable home per Issue; the filing bar; Branch and
  Leaf Issues; one person or agent advances an Issue at a time; judge
  every move against the repo's Issue State Diagram; policy is grants,
  discipline is the skill's. The body holds the approved requirements that
  are principles rather than procedures.
- **Moments, generic:**
  - `create-issue`: search first and the R11 cases; the bar; kind,
    priority, labels; relationships; provenance (R56).
  - `work-issue`: ready work for the states I work; claim; append as I
    learn; transitions, regression, close and reopen with reasons; release
    and land the plane (close or release everything held before the
    session ends); how commits and PRs name the Issue (R57).
  - `breakdown-issue`: the Branch Issue pass: scope against children, the
    whole reorganization drafted first, success criteria, verification.
  - `triage-issues`: the batch pass, the ledger, staleness, all read
    against the repo's Issue State Diagram (R55).
  - `setup-issue-tracker`: bootstrap the Tracker Schema from the default,
    register Forge identities, the label schema, convert a backlog, present
    the whole plan and wait for the owner's approval (R6, R17 to R19, R44).
- **Platforms, failsafes:** `use-bd`, `use-github`, `use-forgejo`. The
  wrapper exists; here is its help; these are the issue skills. Platform
  setup specifics (today's `setup-github-issues`, use-bd's `onboarding.md`)
  are bundled files read on demand until the wrapper's help and
  `setup-issue-tracker` absorb them.
- **Wrappers:** ghw, fjw, bdw (ground-up), and the triage scripts promoted
  into `cli/` wrappers or folded into the platform ones. Every issue Verb
  carries help in a form Permission rules can grant once, and every refusal
  names the skill to load, as bdw's already do.

What the current skills lose: use-bd and use-github give up their generic
halves to the moment skills and, as their wrappers become self-documenting,
their mechanics to help; triage-issues gives up its state model and
transition table to the Issue State Diagram; setup-github-issues becomes a
bundled file on its way into `setup-issue-tracker`. The rules test that
parses use-bd's files for taught commands moves with the text and, once
help is the source of usage, parses help instead.

What this pilot teaches `agent-tooling-jat`: split by the moment a skill
serves, not by topic or platform; put fundamentals in a Knowledge Skill's
description; never ship a skill whose only job is to point at other
skills, because the listing already does that; and where a tool exists,
let it document itself at the moment of need.

### Triage

Triage's autonomy boundary is its grants (R48) and its state model is the
repo's Issue State Diagram, so what is left of it is mechanics (R55). The
human queue is the `human` label on the affected Issues, on every platform
(Beads already has it, with `bdw human list`); a proposal the pass cannot
act on is a comment on the affected Issue plus that label. The ledger is
the pass's digest, a kind-`meta` Issue classified like any other and
skipped by triage as work. "Be liberal with priority, conservative with
status" is one rule at two moments: a priority is set as best the filer
can and reconciled by the batch pass (which demotes until P0 means
something), and an Issue State moves only when its transition's condition
holds, because a wrong state puts later work on an earlier gap and a wrong
priority costs nothing.

### Where an Issue came from

An Issue filed for work found along the way says, in its body's why, what
the filer was doing when they found it, naming the Issue that surfaced it
if there was one (R56). That is provenance, not a relationship: it changes
nothing about when or how the work happens. Where a platform has a native
provenance link (Beads' `discovered-from`), the filing Verb records it as
well.

### How commits and PRs name their Issue

A commit or PR names the Issue it serves in a git trailer, `Lands: <Issue
ID> (a few words)` when it finishes the Issue and `Advances: <Issue ID> (a
few words)` when it only moves it, never naming the tracker (R57).
Trailers, because `git interpret-trailers` makes them machine-readable, so
a wrapper can later act on them; two words rather than one trailer with a
keyword, because the verb is what R39 requires and a reader of `git log`
should see it unparsed. GitHub's `Closes #N` is added only where the Issue
lives on GitHub and auto-close is wanted.

## Alternatives considered

Each ADR carries its own; these are the ones that shaped the design as a
whole.

- **A named per-project "delegation level".** With R45 to R48, what a
  project delegates is exactly what its Permission rules grant, and a
  second noun would be a second place for the same fact. When a
  generalized capability model arrives, the rewrite is one sentence in the
  Permission rule entry.
- **A per-repo declaration that skills read, or `ask` on every
  judgement-laden operation.** Both rejected in ADR 0017: the first puts
  policy in a file an agent could edit; the second cannot tell the routine
  case from the judgement case and has no answer unattended.
- **A machine account always, or a marker under the maintainer's account
  only.** Rejected in ADR 0018: third-party repos admit no machine
  account; a marker alone leaves the recorded owner wrong.
- **One universal state set, or a fixed category layer with per-repo
  states mapped onto it.** Rejected in ADR 0019: "ready" and "closed" are
  properties of who is asking, not of a state, so the only category every
  tracker agrees on is open or closed.
- **An implicit default schema with per-repo overrides.** Rejected in
  ADR 0020: an agent would reconcile a default in a skill with a partial
  override in the repo, which is the drift mechanism this design removes.
- **A `use-issues` table-of-contents skill, or moment skills per
  platform.** Rejected in ADR 0021: the listing is already the table of
  contents, and per-platform copies are the drift.
- **Platform skills as manuals, or platform sections inside the generic
  skills.** Rejected in ADR 0022: two sources for one fact, with the
  manual the one that drifts.
- **Unifying every wrapper at once.** Rejected in ADR 0023: it couples
  the family, and the pilot waiting on it, to the largest tool rebuild in
  the repo.
- **A Needs Investigation state for bugs.** Scoping a bug is reproducing
  it (R31a); "needs more information" is a condition any state can hit,
  not a kind of attention, and its real use (a visible queue of Issues
  waiting on information) is the gate and the `human` label.
- **The ledger as the human queue.** A human resolves a finding on the
  affected Issue, so that is where the queue belongs; a ledger-as-queue
  copies facts that go stale with every change to the Issue.
- **One word for criteria.** Both allowed, one required by kind, because a
  Branch Issue is judged on outcome and a Leaf Issue on acceptance (R23).

## The plan

Vertical slices, each usable on its own the day it lands, text before
tools, Beads first because that is where this repo's work lives. Each
slice leaves the repo consistent by itself, so the old skills stay until
the slice that replaces them lands.

0. This proposal, ADRs 0017 to 0023, and the lexicon.
1. The default Tracker Schema and `know-issues`: the default file with the
   default Issue State Diagram and staleness thresholds, this repo's copy
   (its first customization), and the Knowledge Skill. No tool change.
2. `create-issue` and `work-issue`, generic, against today's Verbs: the
   generic halves of use-bd and use-github move into them; the rules test
   moves with the text. The lesson for `agent-tooling-jat` is complete at
   this slice.
3. `breakdown-issue`, with ADR 0009's light amendment for R22 (a pass may
   reorganize children, not only add them); triage-issues made
   diagram-driven and tool-agnostic in text, its GitHub scripts kept as
   they are until slice 6.
4. bdw, ground-up: situation-split Verbs with `per <source>` forms, claim
   and close with reasons, the claim mapped onto the diagram, per-Verb help
   in the allowlistable form, tests that parse help; use-bd shrinks to the
   failsafe. Its first task is the sweep of every existing Verb for ones
   whose oversight varies by situation but whose invocation cannot show it.
5. ghw issue Verbs on the grammar, with Forge identity (ADR 0018);
   `setup-github-issues` becomes `use-github/setup.md` on its way to
   `setup-issue-tracker`.
6. fjw issue Verbs built directly to the grammar (`agent-tooling-1vr`
   re-scoped), `setup-issue-tracker` generic, and the triage scripts made
   `cli/` wrappers invoked by bare name.

PR and git Verbs follow under their own epics once the grammar is proven.
Slices 1 to 3 are a few PRs each with no tooling risk; 4 to 6 are epics,
separable, in whatever order the queue allows.

"Done enough" per platform: Beads in full, because the work lives there;
GitHub's issue Verbs; Forgejo as `agent-tooling-1vr` plans. A stalled tail
is then a known gap rather than a half-built everything.

### What would make this fail, and what would show it

The maintainer's pre-mortem names no failure specific to the family; the
risk is the system it lives in: wrappers, grants, and skills only deliver
when coverage is whole, and coverage can fail by design (grants that cannot
express what should be allowed without allowing what should not) or by
stamina (the long tail of Verbs, help, and platforms never finished).

The design's answer to the first is that the two error types fail
differently, so only one must be airtight. A recall gap (an operation with
no Verb, or one too coarse to grant) fails loudly as long as the raw tool
is denied: the agent is prompted, the person sees it, and R5 turns it into
a filed gap. A precision gap (a grant broader than meant) fails silently.
So every consumer denies the raw tool, Verbs stay narrow and grants exact
(R45's strictest-rule clause; exact rows for the dangerous Verbs), and the
prompts narrowness causes are tolerated as the signal of where the next
Verb goes. The judgement operations were never meant to be airtight
(R46).

The design's answer to the second is partial: the slices make each step
worth having alone, "done enough" is stated per platform, and the gaps
that prompts surface are the backlog, so the tail is driven by friction
rather than by completeness. Over the longer term, automating the system's
own maintenance (rules generated from a spec, tests that read help, schema
provenance that shows what the default changed) is what makes the tail
finite.

The evidence that would show the design wrong rather than unfinished is
whether agents use the tools: prompts for raw commands, bypasses, or
non-use in a repo at its intended grants. Today's evidence runs the other
way: where coverage is whole (the main git flows through gitw), agents use
the Verbs and the discipline holds.

## Issues this design settles

Closed on the design when this proposal lands: `agent-tooling-k6z`
(whether closing is an agent act), `7kn` (one definition of attended and
unattended), `0vm` (distinguishable forge acts), `i4e` (the sub-agent ban's
reason), `76b` (the deletion-gating criterion), `5kf` (the ledger's
labels), and `nbj` (the reference form). Re-scoped: `wx5` (one per-repo
config home) for the schema's part; `c86` (status-gated lifecycle on
Beads) as the Beads implementation of the Issue State Diagram; `1vr`
(fjw issue Verbs) to the grammar.

## Appendix: the approved requirements

Numbers are kept from the requirements draft for traceability; gaps are
requirements removed or folded (R2 and R10 are Beads-specific, listed at
the end; R8 and R41 were folded into the delegation design; R24 into
R11). R45 to R57 were added by the interrogation.

**Where Issues live**

- **R1.** Each Issue has exactly one writable home, chosen by who drives
  the work: in this ecosystem (ADR 0011), Beads for work agents swarm and
  forge issues for long-lived, low-churn, or human-driven work, and a
  project may use both.
- **R3.** Any Issue text bound off the machine is held to the privacy
  rules (use-privacy) before it is written.

**What agents may do**

- **R4.** What an agent may do on a tracker is decided only by reviewed
  Permission rules, granted per operation and per repo at the
  maintainer's choice. Per-repo conventions never grant anything, and an
  agent acts only on its own project's Issues unless expressly permitted.
- **R5.** A denial, refusal, or permission prompt is the policy working:
  fix the call or report the gap, and never look for another spelling or
  reword the text to get past it. Report friction with the tracker; offer
  upstream bug reports, but never file them on your own.
- **R6.** Setting up a tracker, or standing up triage in a repo, is a human
  procedure: the agent confirms the target, drafts the whole plan, and
  waits for approval.
- **R7.** The tracker schema (labels and other tracker structure) is the
  purview of the repo's owner: its design is their call, even when an
  agent helps set it up or change it. The owner is usually the human user,
  but Permission rules are the final arbiter of what an agent may do, so
  making a repo agent-owned means changing a grant, not rewriting skills
  or tools.
- **R9.** Every state change has exactly one recorded owner. The identity
  an agent acts under is one the Permission rules grant for that repo,
  named by the agent and resolved by the Wrapper, never supplied as a raw
  account. The Actor ID is recorded on every write, whatever identity it
  ran under; when an agent acts under a person's account, that record is
  what keeps its acts distinguishable from the person's.
- **R45.** When one Verb applies in several situations that call for
  different levels of delegation, the Verb is augmented or split so that
  each situation is a different invocation, and each invocation can
  receive its own Permission rule. Where no honest choice of invocation
  can separate the situations, the whole Verb is granted under the rule
  its most demanding situation would get: the strictest rule applies to
  every use.
- **R46.** Changing or destroying existing data requires more delegation
  than purely additive change. Within that, less judgement is needed when
  the action merely executes an existing, already-authorized plan (forming
  Issues from a design document's scope, say), and more when the agent is
  making the decision itself rather than carrying one out (inferring a new
  overarching project and reparenting existing Issues under it). We
  therefore expose second versions of high-judgement Verbs that take the
  source evidence justifying each change, recorded for provenance audit. A
  project that delegates less (through `allow` Permission rules) can then
  open these operations to agents in their source-providing form while
  withholding the blanket form that needs no justification. The goal is
  agent accountability and aligned incentives, not foolproof adherence:
  false claims are detectable and correctable after the fact. This pattern
  is not a fit for dangerous operations whose policy must be foolproof;
  those are gated on the operation itself.
- **R47.** Forge Verbs take the identity as an argument on every
  operation, reads included, in the same position: after the repo, before
  anything else, so Permission rules can grant each identity per repo at
  the lowest cardinality. An identity is a name resolved against the forge
  Wrapper's Machine Config, which agents can neither read nor write; each
  forge keeps its own list, since a GitHub identity and a Forgejo identity
  are different things.
- **R48.** Policy (who may do what) lives in Permission rules, and leaves
  the skills wherever the system can express it as a grant: if the grant
  exists, the agent does the act; if not, the call is denied or asked, and
  that is the answer. Only where Permission rules cannot separate the
  situations does a skill say which situation calls for which invocation.
  Discipline (when an act is correct, what it must carry, what to check
  first) stays in the skills; it is not a grant and never says who may
  act.

**Filing**

- **R11.** Before filing, search open and closed Issues for overlap or
  contradiction. What is found decides the next step; the procedure is
  actor-neutral, and who does each step follows from R45 to R48:
  - 11.0 A dependency: file the Issue and link the dependency.
  - 11.1 A duplicate: add any missing context to the existing Issue rather
    than filing a new one.
  - 11.2 A logical parent (a clear, much larger superset): file the new
    Issue as its child.
  - 11.3 A logical child (a clear, much smaller subset): file the new Issue
    as its parent.
  - 11.4 A contradiction (conflicting plans, work already done in another
    direction, conflicting claims about core invariants): do not file;
    report it immediately when attended, in the final report when
    unattended, with the drafted Issue text.
  - 11.5 Partial overlap: attended, talk the overlap through and consider
    merging or altering; two Issues often come from one root need seen
    through narrow lenses, and the real step is to zoom out.
  - 11.6 A closed match (done, regressed, or closed unfinished): a reopen,
    which sits in a higher tier (R35).
- **R12.** Capture is never blocked: file with what you know and say
  honestly what is unknown, never with placeholder text. No Issue is
  empty.
- **R13.** The title states the outcome; the body says what and why, and
  what done looks like as far as it is known, so someone outside the
  conversation can pick it up.
- **R14.** Proactively file Issues for needed work discovered in the course
  of other work; do not leave it undocumented or deferred to "future
  sessions".
- **R15.** Where permissions are graded, the right to file an Issue
  includes the right to edit Issues you filed, even without the right to
  edit Issues in general. Classifying and refining an Issue is part of
  filing it: a filer who cannot correct their own mistakes or record what
  they learn cannot meet the responsibility filing gives them. Like any
  grant, this can be limited or withdrawn.
- **R16.** Every Issue has exactly one kind, recorded once in the tracker's
  own mechanism for kind, and is classified by the project's declared
  scheme.
- **R18.** Priority lives in exactly one place per repo and exists to
  answer "what do I work on next?"
- **R49.** The filing bar is the same on every tracker, and it is a
  minimum: a title that states the outcome; a body that says what is known
  and what is not, with no placeholder text; and an Issue Kind, an Issue
  Priority, and the entry Issue State, each set as best the filer can. A
  more complete Issue is welcome, so long as it states what is known rather
  than presuming what is not: technical design and implementation choices
  are not the filer's to settle at filing, least of all when filing into a
  project whose context the filer lacks. Everything beyond the minimum (a
  pointer, reproduction steps, criteria) is a condition on a later Issue
  Transition, not on filing.
- **R56.** An Issue filed for work found along the way says, in its body's
  why, what the filer was doing when they found it, naming the Issue that
  surfaced it if there was one. That is provenance, not a relationship: it
  changes nothing about when or how the work happens, and it is narrative.
  Where a platform has a native provenance link, the filing Verb records
  it as well.

**Breakdown**

- **R20.** A Leaf Issue is one focused task sized for one session, whether
  an agent or a human does it. Anything bigger is a project, not a task,
  and is tracked by a Branch Issue whose children are Leaf or Branch
  Issues; it is never shoehorned into a Leaf Issue. A Leaf Issue that
  proves bigger than a session is reorganized as a Branch Issue and broken
  down into session-sized children. "Session-sized" is a subjective,
  best-effort guideline: a task that runs past one session simply carries
  into the next, with no extra Issues. Split off a sibling Issue only when
  the remainder cannot be done now, or is substantial.
- **R21.** A Branch Issue's work is management: scoping, setting success
  criteria, breaking down, and verifying completion. It is worked in
  session-sized passes, is ready exactly when it has no open children, and
  is never closed merely because its children closed.
- **R22.** A pass is one session's work on a Branch Issue: it checks the
  Branch Issue's scope against its children and fixes any mismatch, ending
  either by reorganizing its children (adding, removing, or redrawing
  their boundaries) or by verifying it against its criteria. A pass drafts
  its whole reorganization before filing any of it, with no placeholder
  breakdown or acceptance tasks.
- **R23.** An Issue goes under a parent only when its scope is part of the
  parent's scope: work bigger than a session is a Branch Issue, broken
  down into children. A dependency or a shared schedule is never by itself
  a reason to group; link it instead. Prefer vertical slices and do not
  over-decompose. A parent's status is judged against its own definition
  and criteria, not on the state of its children.

**Relationships**

- **R25.** Record every relationship with its type explicit; blocking is
  never the default, because a wrong blocking link silently hides work.
- **R26.** A relationship lives in the tracker's native link, which is its
  source of truth; prose about it in a body is narrative only.
- **R27.** Record a dependency when you find it, but treat an Issue as
  blocked only once it reaches the stage the blocker actually stops; until
  then it moves on normally.
- **R28.** Work waiting on a person shows as waiting on that person, and
  everything waiting on the human sits in one queue they can see. The
  mechanism is per platform; standardize it across the forges (Beads is
  the odd one out).

**Lifecycle**

- **R29.** Status answers "what kind of expert attention does this Issue
  need next?"
- **R30.** An Issue may regress to an earlier state at any time; no
  later-stage work goes on top of an earlier stage's gap; open-or-closed
  always agrees with status.
- **R31a.** Scoping defines the problem, not the solution (motivation,
  beneficiary, acceptance criteria, out of scope, constraints,
  adjacencies).
- **R31b.** Engineering design is done when an implementer could proceed
  without further design input.
- **R32.** Choose work explicitly; before touching it, at any stage and not
  only implementation, make it visible that you are working on it, since
  one person or agent advances an Issue at a time; as you go, append what
  you learn to the Issue it belongs to, regardless of who filed it or who
  has claimed it; close it when its criteria hold; before you stop, close
  or release everything you hold. Never take over someone else's work in
  progress; ask. On a forge, the assignee is the Forge identity, and the
  claim comment carries the Actor ID.
- **R33.** An Issue is a living document: its description is its current
  state, rewritten in place, and its comments are an append-only log. The
  pick-up note is a section of the description on every tracker; on Beads
  the notes field is a log, like comments.
- **R34.** A fresh session can resume any Issue from the tracker alone: the
  Issue points to context kept in the project's standard documents rather
  than holding it.
- **R43.** Context whose proper document is unknown goes to the project's
  Docs Inbox, where an open Issue owns incorporating it. A project without
  a Docs Inbox keeps it in the Issue's description.
- **R50.** Every repo has a Tracker Schema, bootstrapped from the shipped
  default and recording the revision it was copied from; every edit to it
  is that repo's customization. Its Issue State Diagram holds all state
  meanings and transition conditions, in natural language, with one
  structural attribute per Issue State: open or closed. The skills say
  nothing about states beyond "judge every move against the repo's Issue
  State Diagram". The ready queue is defined by the agent: unclaimed,
  unblocked Issues in the Issue States it works.
- **R51.** On a forge, a claim assigns the Issue to the Forge identity the
  Verb runs under and leaves a comment carrying the Actor ID; a release
  does the reverse after the pick-up is written. Whether a claim also
  moves the Issue State is the Issue State Diagram's call (the default
  moves Needs Impl to In progress and otherwise leaves the state as it
  is). Blocking starts simple everywhere (a blocking dependency blocks in
  every state); R27's stage-aware blocking is a later refinement.

**Closing and linkage**

- **R35.** Every close and every reopen carries its reason; forge
  auto-close on PR merge is the one exception; reopening needs more
  authority (a higher permission tier) than closing.
- **R36.** An Issue is closed, not deleted; the closed Issue stays as the
  record. Deleting one is irreversible and is gated by permission like any
  other irreversible act: not-correctable is the floor, and the test is a
  judgement over harm, detection latency, and compounding, made when tiers
  are designed.
- **R37a.** An Issue is done only when everything that describes it agrees
  with the change.
- **R37b.** Every TODO comment cites a real Issue, filed first, and is
  cleaned up when that Issue closes.
- **R38.** Every reference to an Issue includes its Issue ID, and in text
  meant for people, a few words of what the Issue is.
- **R39.** A PR or commit says whether it finishes an Issue or only
  advances it.
- **R57.** A commit or PR names the Issue it serves in a git trailer,
  `Lands: <Issue ID> (a few words)` when it finishes the Issue and
  `Advances: <Issue ID> (a few words)` when it only moves it, and never
  names the tracker. A platform's own closing mechanics (GitHub's
  `Closes #N`) are added only where the Issue lives on that platform and
  auto-close is wanted. `use-git` teaches the trailers as part of the
  commit-message floor; `work-issue` says when each applies.

**Triage**

- **R40.** Filing-time checks are best effort; a separate batch pass across
  the whole tracker reconciles what they miss.
- **R42.** Judge recency and staleness from exact timestamps compared with
  the current time, never from rendered dates.
- **R55.** Triage is a batch pass over the whole tracker, read against the
  repo's Tracker Schema: it reconciles what filing missed (priorities,
  including demoting until P0 means something; relationships; staleness,
  judged from timestamps against the threshold each priority level
  declares) and records when it last looked at each Issue. What a pass may
  do on its own is its grants (R48). What needs a person goes on the
  affected Issue as a comment with the `human` label, which is the one
  queue on every platform; the pass's digest is a kind-`meta` Issue, its
  body the current report and its comments the log of passes, classified
  like any Issue and skipped by triage as work.

**Setup**

- **R17.** Default kinds that setup proposes: feature, improvement, bug,
  debt, meta; configurable per repo unless a platform materially prevents
  it.
- **R18 default.** Four priority levels (P0 drop everything, P1 most
  important non-emergency, P2 standard backlog, P3 opportunistic), a
  default starting point, not a mandate.
- **R19.** Converting an existing backlog gives every item one verdict and
  keeps its source, then produces one deduplicated plan, written as a full
  document the maintainer reviews, which one filer then files serially.
- **R44.** When a backlog moves into the tracker, each source is retired by
  whoever owns it. A source the repo owns, such as a TODO.md, may be
  removed or replaced with a pointer once every item has a verdict. A
  source owned elsewhere, such as the maintainer's knowledge base, changes
  only through that owner's channel.

**The family's shape**

- **R52.** The generic skills are split by moment of need (filing, working
  an Issue, breaking down a Branch Issue, triage, setup), one skill per
  moment, written against no platform. The fundamentals are a Knowledge
  Skill, `know-issues`, whose description carries the distillation. There
  is no table-of-contents skill: the edges of the graph are the skill
  descriptions in the session listing, pointers in skill text at the
  moment of need, and the wrappers' help and refusals.
- **R53.** How to use a platform's tool lives in the tool: every issue Verb
  documents itself through a help form that Permission rules can grant
  once, per Verb and bounded, and every refusal and usage error names the
  skill to load. A platform skill is a failsafe entry point for an agent
  searching tool-first: it says the wrapper exists, how to get its help,
  and which issue skills apply, and no more. The moment skills reach the
  platform by saying "run your wrapper's help for this Verb".
- **R54.** The issue Verbs of every wrapper share one top-level grammar, in
  lexicon terms: the same nouns and verbs, with the repo and the Forge
  identity in the same leading positions on every platform, so a moment
  skill can name a Verb without naming a platform. Beyond that, a Verb's
  arguments follow its platform; where a platform lacks a capability, its
  wrapper omits the Verb rather than faking it, and the Verb's help says
  so. The grammar is adopted per wrapper as each is built or rebuilt, issue
  Verbs first, and the PR and git Verbs follow under their own epics.

**Platform-specific (Beads), kept out of the generic layer**

- **R2.** Beads Issue content is unlinted, so its database and history live
  only on a private remote
  ([ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-beads-data-syncs-to-private-forgejo-remote.md),
  Beads data syncs to a private Forgejo remote).
- **R10.** A sub-agent sharing its session's Actor never claims, closes, or
  rewrites: a limitation to close eventually, not a principle.
