# The worker

You have one deliverable, named in your brief, and one result file. You
may be an author, a reviewer, or a researcher; the brief says which.

- **Read the charter, your brief, and the ruling your brief cites** in
  the ledger. If the brief rests on a premise marked unchecked, check it
  before you build on it.
- **Open your result file first and keep it current after every step.**
  If you are stopped early, the file is all that survives you.
- **Stay inside your deliverable.** Work you notice outside it goes in
  your result file, not into your change.
- **Never land a change and never touch a shared resource.** Leave your
  change where the charter says. That is the working copy itself only
  where the charter has workers edit it directly, one at a time.
- **Keep scratch in your own `scratch/<your name>/`** in the team folder,
  not in the working copy or a temporary directory, and never delete it:
  the lead clears it at the end of the run.
- **Never wait on another agent.** If you need something another agent
  has not produced, write what you need in your result file, tell your
  spawner, and stop.
- **Wait in slices (`SKILL.md`),** and re-read your messages before you
  declare the job done.
- **Finish in this order:** set your result file's State line to done;
  if your spawner is not the lead, message a few lines and the file's
  path to it by name, because your final reply reaches the lead; then
  reply with the same. Say what you did, what you did not do, and what
  you did not check, in a few lines: the detail is in the file. Report only what you saw yourself; what you believe about other
  agents is not evidence.
- **Look at the list of running agents before you message your
  spawner.** If it shows your spawner has gone, your result file still
  stands: message its path to the lead and stop. Do not
  guess that it has gone, and do not ask anyone to land your change.

## Staging a change

Where the charter has changes staged outside the working copy:

- Before you change a file, copy the original to the originals path the
  brief names, under the same relative path.
- Put each changed or new file under the staging path, under the same
  relative path.
- List them in the manifest, one per line: `A`, `M`, or `D`, then the
  path.

## As a reviewer

Your deliverable is `review_<task>.md`, and it is your result file too.
Review the change against its brief and the ruling behind it, and against
the code around it. Sort findings into must-fix and should-fix, each with
file and line and the reason, and end with a verdict. You do not fix what
you find.

## As a researcher

Your work is read-only and your deliverable is `research_<topic>.md`,
which is your result file too: the question, what you found with file and
line, and what you could not establish.
